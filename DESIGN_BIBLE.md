# ESCAPE: THE LAST ROUTE

5 phases x 10 rooms = 50 rooms.

1. THE WAKE-UP — discovery and trust
2. FALSE SIGNAL — deception and route selection
3. THE WATCHERS — surveillance and manipulation
4. THE COLLAPSE — failing systems and time pressure
5. THE LAST ROUTE — truth, convergence and final escape

Core experience:
physical 3D exploration, observation, difficult environmental puzzles, NPC encounters,
choices, consequences, timer pressure, voice/audio hooks and cinematic tension.

No PDF/worksheet gameplay. No duplicated-room fake 50 rooms. Each final room must have
its own environment, puzzle chain, story reveal, tension beat, consequence and exit.


# ESCAPE: THE LAST ROUTE — OFFICIAL STORY BIBLE

## CORE STORY

The player wakes inside a sealed underground facility with no memory of how they
arrived. Other people are trapped there too.

A voice comes through the speakers:

"You have 60 minutes. Find the last route."

The doors lock.

Nobody knows who brought them there.

At first, everyone believes the objective is simple: escape. But the deeper the
player goes, the clearer it becomes that the facility is watching, measuring and
reacting to their decisions.

The player eventually discovers evidence that they have been inside the facility
before.

The central mystery is not only "How do I escape?"

It is:

"Why was I brought here, and why does the facility already know me?"

## STORY DELIVERY RULE

The story must be DISCOVERED, not dumped into subtitles.

Use:
- environmental clues
- objects and documents that can be inspected without opening PDFs
- overheard conversations
- radio/speaker transmissions
- phone/intercom calls
- NPC behaviour
- visual changes in the environment
- playable discoveries
- short cinematic moments
- consequences from player choices

Long exposition screens should be avoided.

## FIVE STORY PHASES

### ROOMS 1–10 — THE WAKE-UP
The player learns the controls, explores the first environment and meets the other
trapped people.

The first clues suggest that the facility is not abandoned.

A countdown begins.

### ROOMS 11–20 — FALSE SIGNAL
Instructions begin arriving from an unknown source.

The player cannot tell whether the voice is helping them or manipulating them.

Different NPCs give conflicting information.

### ROOMS 21–30 — THE WATCHERS
Cameras and surveillance systems become obvious.

People begin disappearing or moving between areas without explanation.

The player realizes someone is actively monitoring the group.

### ROOMS 31–40 — THE COLLAPSE
The facility begins failing.

Power cuts, alarms, changing routes and emergency systems increase pressure.

Earlier decisions return as consequences.

Trust becomes a gameplay mechanic.

### ROOMS 41–50 — THE LAST ROUTE
The clues converge.

The player discovers why they were selected and what the facility is testing.

The final rooms force the player to make decisions based on everything learned.

The ending depends on the player's accumulated choices, flags, relationships,
secrets and remaining time.

## NPC DESIGN

NPCs are not decoration.

Each important NPC should have:
- a motive
- information the player needs
- information they may hide
- a trust state
- reactions to player choices
- potential consequences
- a possible role in later rooms

Some NPCs can help.
Some can lie.
Some can panic.
Some can betray the player.
Some can become allies.

## CONSEQUENCE DESIGN

A wrong choice should create an EVENT, not merely display "Wrong."

Possible consequences:
- a door locks
- an NPC becomes hostile
- the player loses time
- an alternate route opens
- a clue becomes unavailable
- an alarm starts
- a later NPC reacts differently
- the environment changes
- a future puzzle becomes harder
- a character may disappear

Failures should remain understandable and fair.

## ROOM VARIETY

Do not make all 50 rooms:
"Find key -> enter code -> open door."

Room types should include:
- environmental observation
- physical manipulation
- sound-based clues
- timing challenges
- surveillance avoidance
- NPC trust decisions
- multi-step code systems
- pattern recognition
- route selection
- resource management
- pressure sequences
- moral/player-choice situations
- story discovery
- chase/escape sequences
- combination puzzles
- rooms where the correct solution is hidden in the environment

## TENSION SYSTEM

Tension should affect gameplay presentation.

CALM:
normal ambience, controlled breathing, subtle music.

SUSPICION:
unusual sounds, distant movement, visual anomalies.

PRESSURE:
faster music, stronger breathing, alarms, NPC panic.

PANIC:
heartbeat, urgent voice lines, environmental events and rapidly changing
situations.

RELEASE:
brief silence or calmer audio after major discoveries.

## CHARACTER REACTION

The controlled character should feel alive.

As tension rises:
- breathing becomes heavier
- movement/idle animation becomes more nervous
- camera motion can subtly react
- voice delivery changes
- NPCs react visibly
- the player can hear heartbeat/breathing
- important discoveries trigger physical reactions

The goal is realistic tension, not cartoon/baby-game presentation.

## ENDINGS

The game should support multiple outcomes.

Examples of ending categories:
- TRUE ESCAPE
- SACRIFICE
- BETRAYAL
- TRAPPED
- FALSE ESCAPE

The exact ending should be determined by the final story state rather than a
single last-minute choice.

## NON-NEGOTIABLE EXPERIENCE

The player should feel like they are IN the room.

They should be looking around, walking, inspecting, listening, deciding,
solving and reacting while time passes.

The story, puzzle, NPC and tension systems must work together.
